What's different in California

California is one of only four jurisdictions enforcing strict statutory daily thresholds in addition to federal workweek limits.

  • 1. Daily 8-hour threshold1

    California mandates 1.5× pay after 8 hours in a workday, unlike the federal FLSA which only activates after 40 cumulative weekly hours.

    1 Cal. Lab. Code § 510(a); IWC Wage Orders 1–16.
  • 2. Double time after 12 hours2

    Work beyond 12 hours in any single workday must be compensated at not less than twice (2.0×) the employee's regular rate of pay.

    2 California Division of Labor Standards Enforcement, DLSE Manual § 49.1.2.
  • 3. The 7th consecutive day rule3

    An employee working seven days in a workweek receives 1.5× for the first 8 hours on the seventh day, and 2.0× for all hours worked past 8.

    3 Cal. Lab. Code § 510(a); dir.ca.gov enforcement guidance.

Worked example: Calculating Wednesday

On Wednesday, the employee worked a long 12.5-hour shift. Under standard federal rules, that day is simply counted toward the cumulative 40-hour threshold. Under California Labor Code § 510, however, that single shift must be compartmentalized into three distinct legal tranches:

Regular rate (hours 0 through 8.0): 8.0h × $20.00 = $160.00
Overtime rate (hours 8.0 through 12.0): 4.0h × $30.00 = $120.00
Double-time rate (hours 12.0 to 12.5): 0.5h × $40.00 = $20.00
Single-day Wednesday gross: $260.00

Because daily overtime hours have already been compensated with statutory premiums, they are credited against weekly overtime to avoid double-pyramiding liabilities.

Frequently asked questions

How does the 7th consecutive day rule work? ↓

When an employee is required to work all 7 consecutive days in a defined payroll workweek, the first 8 hours on Day 7 are legally paid at 1.5×. Any time exceeding 8 hours on that seventh day must be paid at 2.0×.

Can employers agree to waive daily overtime? ↓

No individual agreement can waive California statutory overtime. The only permissible exception is a formal Alternative Workweek Schedule (AWS) approved by a secret-ballot two-thirds vote of affected employees (e.g. four 10-hour days without overtime).

How do bonuses affect the regular rate? ↓

Under California DLSE rules, non-discretionary bonuses (e.g. attendance or production bonuses) must be factored into the base wage. California divides non-hourly flat bonuses by regular hours worked (maximum 40), yielding a higher regular rate than FLSA.

Does California allow comp time in lieu of pay? ↓

Private employers in California cannot offer compensatory time off ("comp time") instead of monetary overtime wages. Overtime premiums must be settled in full on regular semi-monthly or bi-weekly paydays.
