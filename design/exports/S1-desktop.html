Tip pooling regulations by jurisdiction

State statutes supersede federal defaults whenever state requirements provide greater employee wage protection.

California (Strict Employer Prohibition • No Tip Credits) expand_more

Under California Labor Code § 351, employers and their direct agents (supervisors and managers with authority to hire, fire, or direct work) are strictly forbidden from collecting, taking, or receiving any portion of gratuities.

Furthermore, tip credits against minimum wage are completely unlawful in California; every tipped worker must receive the full state/local minimum wage before any gratuity is paid. Kitchen/BOH staff may participate in mandatory tip pools only if traditional chain-of-service ties exist and supervisory staff are completely excluded1.

Reference: 1 Cal. Lab. Code § 351; Etheridge v. Reins International California, Inc.
New York (Hospitality Wage Order • 80/20 Dual-Job Limits) expand_more

New York Labor Law § 196-d limits tip pooling strictly to employees who perform direct customer service duties. BOH employees (such as line cooks, dishwashers, and expediters) cannot participate in mandatory tip pools if the employer claims a statutory tip credit against minimum wage2.

Reference: 2 NYLL § 196-d; 12 NYCRR Part 146 (Hospitality Industry Wage Order).
Texas (Standard FLSA 29 U.S.C. Adoption • Notice Rule) expand_more

Texas adheres to the federal Fair Labor Standards Act provisions. Employers may claim the standard maximum tip credit up to $5.12/hr (paying $2.13/hr in direct cash wages), provided employees are formally notified in advance and retain 100% of all gratuities received3.

Reference: 3 FLSA 29 U.S.C. § 203(m); Texas Labor Code Chapter 62.
warning

Statutory Watch Out: Management Participation Is Prohibited

Under federal law (29 U.S.C. § 203(m)(2)(B)), managers and supervisors cannot participate in or take any funds from a tip pool, regardless of whether they physically clear tables, pour drinks, or serve patrons during peak hours. Doing so invalidates the entire pool and exposes the employer to 100% back-wage liquidated damages.

Frequently asked questions

Can salaried or shift managers participate in tip pools?

No. The DOL applies the executive exemption duties test (29 CFR § 541.100). If an employee regularly directs the work of two or more staff members or has input into scheduling/hiring, they are classified as a manager and cannot retain tips from any pooled source.

How are credit card transaction processing fees handled?

In states where permitted (not in CA, CO, or ME), an employer may subtract the actual transactional processing percentage charged by the credit card issuer (e.g. 2.5%) from the tip amount before disbursing it to the pool participant.

What is the distinction between tip pooling and tip sharing?

Tip pooling involves accumulating all tips into a communal fund distributed according to an established mathematical formula. Tip sharing (or tipping out) involves direct, voluntary or customary transfers from primary service workers to support staff (e.g., servers tipping bartenders directly).

How does the FLSA 80/20 dual-job rule affect dual-role staff?

If an employee spends more than 20% of their working hours performing non-tipped support duties (or more than 30 continuous minutes), the employer cannot claim the tip-credit wage rate for those hours and must pay full statutory minimum wage.

Can back-of-house staff receive tip-outs if no tip credit is taken?

Yes. Under the 2020 FLSA amendments, if the employer pays all staff the full minimum wage without utilizing a tip credit, non-supervisory kitchen staff (cooks, dishwashers, cleaners) may participate in mandatory tip pools.
