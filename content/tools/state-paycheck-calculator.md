## How statutory paycheck withholding works

Your take-home paycheck equals gross earnings (regular hourly wages + reported tips + overtime) minus three mandatory withholding deductions:

1. **FICA Taxes (Federal Insurance Contributions Act)**:
   - **Social Security**: 6.2% on total gross earnings up to the annual Social Security wage base ($184,500 for 2026).
   - **Medicare**: 1.45% on all earnings, plus an additional 0.9% for high-income earners exceeding $200,000 (single) or $250,000 (married filing jointly).

2. **Federal Income Tax**:
   - Calculated using annual projected taxable earnings after subtracting standard deductions ($15,700 for single filers, $31,400 for married filing jointly) applied across progressive IRS marginal tax brackets (10%, 12%, 22%, 24%, 32%, 35%, and 37%).

3. **State Income Tax**:
   - Nine states have zero wage income tax (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, New Hampshire, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, and Wyoming).
   - Other states apply either a flat tax rate (e.g. Pennsylvania 3.07%, Illinois 4.95%, Colorado 4.4%) or progressive graduated brackets (e.g. California up to 12.3%, New York up to 10.9%).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are tips subject to both FICA and income taxes?
Yes. Both cash and card tips reported to your employer are subject to federal income tax withholding and FICA payroll taxes (6.2% Social Security and 1.45% Medicare).

### What if my state does not have an income tax?
If you select Texas, Florida, Nevada, Washington, Tennessee, South Dakota, Wyoming, Alaska, or New Hampshire, your estimated state income tax withholding is exactly $0.00.
