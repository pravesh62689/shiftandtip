## Massachusetts Tipped Service Rate & Tip Credit Framework

Massachusetts regulates restaurant, bar, and hospitality wages under Massachusetts General Laws (M.G.L.) Chapter 151, Section 7, and the Massachusetts Tips Act (M.G.L. c. 149, § 152A). Unlike states that ban tip credits entirely, Massachusetts allows employers to pay a lower **service rate** of **$6.75 per hour** to employees who customarily and regularly receive more than $20 per month in tips—provided specific legal conditions are met on every single shift.

If tips received during a shift do not raise the employee's combined hourly earnings (cash wage + tips) to at least the full **$15.00 per hour** Massachusetts minimum wage, the employer must pay the difference as **tip-credit make-up pay**. Crucially, Massachusetts evaluates tip-credit sufficiency on a **shift-by-shift** (daily) basis rather than averaging a slow Tuesday shift against a busy Saturday night.

## Key Massachusetts Wage & Overtime Benchmarks

- **Standard State Minimum Wage**: **$15.00 per hour** (verified against U.S. DOL WHD state schedules).
- **Tipped Service Rate (Cash Wage Floor)**: **$6.75 per hour**.
- **Maximum Tip Credit**: **$8.25 per hour** ($15.00 − $6.75 = $8.25).
- **Daily Shift Make-Up Rule**: Under 454 CMR 27.03(2), total tips + cash wages must average at least $15.00/hr **for each individual shift**, not merely across the whole workweek.
- **Weekly Overtime Threshold**: **40 hours** per workweek at **1.5x** the full minimum wage, minus the tip credit claimed.
- **Minimum Tipped Cash Overtime Rate**: **$14.25 per hour** — calculated as ($15.00 × 1.5) − $8.25 = $22.50 − $8.25 = **$14.25/hr**.

## Why Tipped Overtime in Massachusetts Is $14.25/hr (Not $10.13/hr)

One of the most frequent payroll violations in Massachusetts hospitality is multiplying the $6.75 service rate by 1.5 ($10.125/hr). Both federal FLSA law and Massachusetts law **forbid** taking 1.5x of the subminimum cash wage. Instead, the employer must calculate 1.5x of the **full $15.00 minimum wage** ($22.50/hr) and then subtract the exact **$8.25** hourly tip credit:

- **Full minimum overtime rate**: $15.00 × 1.5 = **$22.50/hr**
- **Minus maximum tip credit**: −$8.25/hr
- **Minimum legal cash overtime rate**: **$14.25/hr**

Paying $10.13/hr instead of $14.25/hr underpays every overtime hour by **$4.125**, which can trigger triple damages under the Massachusetts Wage Act (M.G.L. c. 149, § 150).

## Worked Example 1: Server Working 45 Hours in Boston

Sam works as a server in Boston at the **$6.75/hr** service rate for **45 hours** across five shifts, earning **$720.00** in tips (each shift averages well above $15.00/hr including tips).

Step-by-step payroll calculation:
- **Straight-time cash wages (40 hours)**: 40 × $6.75 = **$270.00**
- **Tipped cash overtime rate**: ($15.00 × 1.5) − $8.25 = **$14.25/hr**
- **Overtime cash wages (5 hours)**: 5 × $14.25 = **$71.25**
- **Total employer-paid cash wages**: $270.00 + $71.25 = **$341.25**
- **Reported tips**: **$720.00**
- **Total weekly gross pay**: $341.25 + $720.00 = **$1,061.25**

If Sam's employer had mistakenly paid 1.5x of $6.75 ($10.13/hr), Sam's overtime pay would have been $50.65 instead of $71.25—a $20.60 underpayment in a single week.

## Worked Example 2: Slow Lunch Shift Requiring Make-Up Pay

Because Massachusetts requires the $15.00/hr average to hold **per shift**, consider a slow 4-hour Tuesday lunch shift where Sam earns only **$18.00** in tips:
- **Cash wages for 4 hours**: 4 × $6.75 = **$27.00**
- **Tips earned on that shift**: **$18.00**
- **Combined shift pay**: $27.00 + $18.00 = **$45.00** ($11.25/hr)
- **Required minimum wage for 4 hours**: 4 × $15.00 = **$60.00**
- **Employer make-up pay owed**: $60.00 − $45.00 = **$15.00**

Even if Sam earned $300 in tips on Friday night, the employer still owes **$15.00** in make-up pay for Tuesday's lunch shift.

## Massachusetts Tips Act: Who Can Share a Tip Pool?

Under M.G.L. c. 149, § 152A (the Massachusetts Tips Act), tip pools are strictly limited to **"wait staff employees, service employees, or service bartenders"** who have **no managerial responsibility**. Because Massachusetts enforces one of the strictest tip-pool statutes in the country:
- **Managers, shift supervisors with hiring/disciplinary authority, and owners** can never receive tips from a pool.
- **Back-of-house (BOH) staff** such as line cooks and dishwashers generally **cannot** participate in a mandatory tip pool under Massachusetts state law when the employer relies on the Tips Act definitions—even if the employer pays the full $15.00 minimum wage.
- **Credit card tip fees** cannot be deducted from tips in Massachusetts if doing so violates the Tips Act or reduces pay below statutory minimums.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is the minimum cash wage for tipped servers in Massachusetts?
The Massachusetts tipped service rate is $6.75 per hour, provided the employee receives at least $20/month in tips and earns at least $15.00/hr combined (cash wage + tips) on every individual shift.

### How much is tipped overtime in Massachusetts?
At the $6.75 service rate, the minimum cash overtime rate is $14.25 per hour for hours worked beyond 40 in a workweek: ($15.00 × 1.5) − $8.25 tip credit = $14.25/hr.

### Can an employer average a slow shift with a busy weekend shift in Massachusetts?
No. Massachusetts regulation 454 CMR 27.03(2) requires the $15.00/hr combined minimum to be met on each shift separately.

### Are mandatory service charges treated as tips in Massachusetts?
Under M.G.L. c. 149, § 152A, any fee labeled as a "service charge" or "tip" (or that a reasonable patron would assume goes to service staff) must be remitted in full to wait staff employees unless clearly disclosed otherwise as an administrative fee.
