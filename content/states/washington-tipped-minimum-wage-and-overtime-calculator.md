## Washington State Tipped Wage & Zero Tip Credit Framework

Washington State enforces one of the strongest baseline wage floors in the United States for hospitality, restaurant, bar, and delivery workers. Under Washington's Minimum Wage Act (RCW 49.46) and Washington Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) rules, **tip credits are strictly prohibited**. Every non-exempt tipped employee must receive at least the full statewide minimum wage of **$17.13 per hour** directly from their employer before any customer gratuities are counted.

Unlike federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) rules—which permit employers in tip-credit states to pay as little as $2.13 per hour in direct cash wages—Washington treats customer tips and voluntary gratuities as 100% supplemental income belonging to the employee. An employer cannot reduce an employee's hourly base rate below $17.13 because of tips, nor can an employer count tips toward meeting the statutory minimum wage obligation.

## Key Washington 2026–2027 Wage & Overtime Benchmarks

- **Statewide Minimum Wage**: **$17.13 per hour** for workers aged 16 and older (verified against U.S. DOL WHD state schedules).
- **Tipped Cash Wage Floor**: **$17.13 per hour** — identical to the standard minimum wage.
- **Maximum Tip Credit Allowed**: **$0.00** (tip credits are unlawful under RCW 49.46.020 and L&I policy).
- **Weekly Overtime Threshold**: **40 hours** per designated seven-day workweek at **1.5x** the employee's regular rate of pay.
- **Base Minimum Overtime Rate**: **$25.70 per hour** ($17.13 × 1.5 = $25.695, rounded to $25.70) before any non-discretionary bonuses or service-charge commissions.
- **Local City Overrides**: Municipalities such as Seattle, SeaTac, Tukwila, Burien, Renton, and Bellingham enforce higher local minimum wages that supersede the $17.13 statewide floor when work is performed inside city limits.

## How Service Charges Differ from Voluntary Tips in Washington

Under RCW 49.46.160, Washington draws a sharp legal distinction between **tips** (voluntary amounts left by customers) and **service charges** (automatic mandatory percentages added to banquets, catering invoices, or large-party restaurant checks):

1. **Disclosure Requirement**: Any employer charging an automatic service charge must disclose on the itemized receipt and menu what percentage of the charge is paid directly to the employee(s) serving the customer.
2. **Default Ownership**: If the employer fails to disclose how the service charge is distributed, the default statutory rule treats it as belonging to the serving staff.
3. **Impact on Overtime Regular Rate**: Unlike voluntary customer tips (which are excluded from the regular rate of pay), any portion of a mandatory service charge paid out to an hourly employee **must be included in their regular rate of pay** when calculating 1.5x weekly overtime.

## Worked Example 1: Full-Time Server Working 46 Hours in Spokane

Jordan works as a server in a Spokane restaurant earning the Washington state base wage of **$17.13 per hour**. During a busy holiday week, Jordan clocks **46 hours** and earns **$680.00** in voluntary customer tips (paid out via POS nightly settlement).

Step-by-step payroll audit:
- **Straight-time hours (first 40 hours)**: 40 hours × $17.13 = **$685.20**
- **Overtime rate (1.5x)**: $17.13 × 1.5 = **$25.695** ($25.70/hr)
- **Overtime hours (hours 41 through 46 = 6 hours)**: 6 hours × $25.695 = **$154.17**
- **Total employer-paid wages**: $685.20 + $154.17 = **$839.37**
- **Voluntary customer tips**: **$680.00** (excluded from overtime base, 100% retained by Jordan minus any lawful tip-pool share)
- **Total weekly gross compensation**: $839.37 + $680.00 = **$1,519.37**

Notice how dramatically Washington's no-tip-credit rule protects tipped workers compared to a federal $2.13/hr state: Jordan receives $839.37 in guaranteed employer wages alone before a single dollar of tips is added.

## Worked Example 2: Banquet Server with Mandatory Service Charges

Priya works 44 hours in Tacoma at **$17.13 per hour** base pay and receives **$220.00** from a disclosed mandatory banquet service charge during the same workweek. Because mandatory service charges distributed to employees count as non-discretionary commission-style compensation, they raise the regular rate for overtime:

- **Base hourly wages (44 hours)**: 44 × $17.13 = **$753.72**
- **Included service-charge pay**: **$220.00**
- **Total straight-time compensation**: $753.72 + $220.00 = **$973.72**
- **Blended regular rate**: $973.72 ÷ 44 hours = **$22.13 per hour**
- **Half-time overtime premium for 4 OT hours**: 4 × ($22.13 × 0.5) = 4 × $11.065 = **$44.26**
- **Total gross pay**: $973.72 + $44.26 = **$1,017.98**

## Common Washington Tipped Payroll Mistakes

- **Deducting credit card processing fees excessively**: Employers in Washington may deduct only the actual proportionate credit card processing fee on the tip portion (if permitted), never a flat inflated percentage that eats into base wages.
- **Including managers in tip pools**: Both federal FLSA regulations and Washington L&I prohibit managers and supervisors from keeping any portion of a tip pool, even though Washington pays the full minimum wage.
- **Ignoring service-charge regular-rate adjustments**: Paying 1.5x only on the $17.13 base rate when a worker also earned mandatory banquet service charges underpays weekly overtime.
- **Missing local city minimums**: A server working inside Seattle city limits must be paid the higher Seattle minimum wage rate rather than $17.13.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a Washington restaurant pay less than $17.13 per hour if servers make great tips?
No. Washington prohibits tip credits statewide. Every hour worked must be paid at least $17.13 per hour (or higher in cities with local minimum wage ordinances) directly by the employer.

### Are tip pools legal in Washington State?
Yes. Employers may require a valid tip pool among non-management staff (such as servers, bartenders, bussers, and kitchen staff), provided no owner, manager, or supervisor takes a share from the pool.

### When does overtime kick in for Washington tipped workers?
Overtime applies after 40 hours worked in a single seven-day workweek. Washington does not mandate daily 8-hour overtime for general hospitality employees, though certain healthcare or union contracts may specify daily premiums.

### Are tips subject to Washington state income tax?
Washington has no state personal income tax on wages or tips. However, all cash and charged tips remain subject to federal income tax and FICA (Social Security and Medicare) payroll taxes, subject to applicable federal tip deduction rules.
