## Minnesota Paycheck Withholding Architecture

Every paycheck issued in Minnesota reflects statutory federal withholdings alongside state-specific income tax rules. For hospitality, shift, and hourly employees who collect tips, computing net take-home requires balancing gross base hourly wages, overtime pay, and reported gratuities against both payroll taxes (FICA) and progressive income tax brackets.

Minnesota applies progressive graduated tax brackets ranging from 5.3% up to 9.8%.

## Statutory Withholding Components in Minnesota

1. **Social Security (FICA)**: Assessed at 6.2% on all compensation up to the statutory 2026 wage base threshold of $184,500. Tips are fully subject to FICA.
2. **Medicare (FICA)**: Assessed at 1.45% across all taxable wages, with an additional 0.9% surtax applying to annual earnings exceeding $200,000 for single filers ($250,000 for married couples).
3. **Federal Income Tax**: Determined by applying your filing status (Single, Married Filing Jointly, or Head of Household) and standard deductions ($15,700 for single filers in 2026) to marginal brackets spanning 10% to 37%.
4. **Minnesota State Income Tax**: Withheld according to official Minnesota Department of Revenue withholding formulas.

## Worked Example 1: Hourly Server with Tips in Minnesota

Jordan works as a server in Minnesota paid on a biweekly payroll schedule (26 pay periods annually). During this pay cycle, Jordan earns $1,600.00 in regular hourly wages and reports $500.00 in customer tips, producing a gross compensation of $2,100.00.

Applying single filing status with standard deductions:
- **Gross Compensation**: $2,100.00
- **Social Security (6.2%)**: −$130.20
- **Medicare (1.45%)**: −$30.45
- **Federal Income Tax**: −$170.37
- **Minnesota State Income Tax**: −$87.01
- **Estimated Net Take-Home Pay**: $1,681.97
- **Effective Tax Rate**: 19.9% (Annualized gross: $54,600.00)

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are credit card tips taxed differently than cash tips in Minnesota?
No. Under federal and state regulations, all tips—whether paid in cash, added to a credit card slip, or distributed through a tip pool—are treated as taxable wage income subject to FICA and income tax withholding.

### How does Minnesota handle overtime taxation?
Overtime wages are taxed as ordinary earned income. While overtime hours are compensated at 1.5 times your regular hourly rate, withholding follows standard federal and Minnesota brackets based on annualized income.

### When does Social Security tax stop being withheld?
Social Security tax ceases once your cumulative gross wages across all employers reach the annual statutory wage base of $184,500 for the tax year.

### Can I adjust my state withholding in Minnesota?
Yes. You can submit an updated state employee withholding certificate or federal Form W-4 to adjust allowances or specify additional withholding per paycheck.
